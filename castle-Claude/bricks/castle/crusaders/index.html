Classic era (1978–1999)

Crusaders / Lion Knights

Lion Knights2022–2025 · 2001 · 1984–199236 sets43 minifigures

The Lion Knights, named, as LEGO.com puts it, after their iconic shield insignia. They arrived officially in 1984 and their empire stretched across blacksmiths, armouries and royal escorts; they owned the largest castle of the time (6080), which could be expanded with sets like 6067 Guarded Inn. According to LEGO History, they and the Black Falcons were the biggest factions of the new castle generation. The 1992 US and Canadian catalogue called them Crusaders; LEGO today uses Lion Knights.

Crest and colours

A blue, red and yellow shield with a rampant lion on a contrasting field (LEGO.com). Red torsos with blue sleeves, grey helmets; some torsos show crossed axes.

Characters
  • No names in the classic era – only “the King” in set titles.
  • King Leo and Cedric the Bull belong to Knights' Kingdom (2000), not here.

Medieval Town Square

2024 · 3,304 pieces · 8 minifigures ○ retired

According to LEGO, the village of Felsa, inspired by the 2009 set 10193 Medieval Market Village and a companion to castle 10305 (designers Henrik Rubin Saaby and Rok Žgalin Kobe). A square with an old tree where villagers trade goods, the tax collector's house with a secret money compartment, wanted posters, a shield painter's workshop finishing a Lion Knights shield, the Broken Axe tavern, a farmhouse with a tame goat, a stall with cheese, pretzels and pastries, and a watchtower where a Lion Knight keeps guard. 10305 · Icons

Lion Knights' Castle

2022 · 4,514 pieces · 22 minifigures ○ retired

The flagship of the classics' return. It was made for LEGO's 90th anniversary at fans' request: the theme was chosen in a LEGO Ideas poll in which, according to LEGO, 55,000 people took part. It is a new version of the 1984 castle 6080 (designers Milan Madge, Mike Psiaki and Carl Merriam), sold only at LEGO.com and LEGO Stores. Closed, it measures 38 × 44 × 33 cm; its two halves open to almost 70 cm. Inside: a stable, an armoury, a dungeon, a kitchen with a mill and a working water wheel, a dining hall and the queen's chamber with a miniature 375 castle. The drawbridge and portcullis can be raised and lowered, moving walls hide secret passages, and beneath the castle is the forest folk's hideout – when they attack, a trapdoor drops them straight into the dungeon, from which they can escape through a secret tunnel. 22 minifigures: the queen and Lion Knights, Black Falcons, Forestmen and the wizard Majisto. The box copies the 1980s LEGOLAND Castle style.

Allies and enemies

  • Arch-enemies of the Black Falcons – LEGO.com describes them as “ready to attack the Black Falcons”. official
  • According to the fan legend retold on LEGO.com, after the Black Falcons' defeat the Black Knight fled and founded the Black Knights. fan reading
  • According to the legend (LEGO.com), King Lionheart renamed the faction Royal Knights in 1995 and updated the crest. fan reading
  • Conflict with the Forestmen – read from set scenes (e.g. 6042). fan reading
  • Reconciled with the Black Falcons: the 10305 box (2022) says the long rivalry “seems to be ending”. official

History

  • 2024–2025: a Lion Knight guards the village in 10332, and the faction appears in BrickLink Designer Program sets.
  • 2022: 10305 Lion Knights' Castle for LEGO's 90th anniversary.
  • 1990: 6081 King's Mountain Fortress with a raised baseplate.
  • 1986: 6067 Guarded Inn and 6041 Armor Shop with civilians; Guarded Inn was re-released in 2001 as 10000.
  • 1984: a new castle generation with one-piece horses, saddles, fixed-visor helmets, plumes, flags and bows (LEGO History); 6080 King's Castle.
